1. What is a resistor?
A resistor is an electronic component that provides resistance to the flow of electric current in a circuit.
- Symbol: R
- Unit: Ohm Omega
- Measuring instrument: Ohmmeter or a multimeter set to resistance mode
- Circuit symbols: A zigzag line or a rectangular box, depending on the drawing standard.





2. 🤔 Why do we need a resistor?
A resistor is used to control current and create a required voltage drop in a circuit.
Its main purposes are:
- 🛡️ Protect components: Limit current to protect LEDs and other sensitive components.
- 🎚️ Control current: Set how much current flows through a circuit.
- ⚡ Drop voltage: Produce a voltage drop when current flows through it.
- 📡 Set signal levels: Help create voltage-divider circuits for sensors and electronic inputs.
- ⏱️ Control timing: Work with capacitors to create timing and filtering circuits.
- 🔥 Produce heat: Convert electrical energy into heat, as in power resistors.
- 🧪 Measure current: A small resistor, called a shunt resistor, can be used to measure current from its voltage drop.
Example: An LED connected directly to a suitable voltage source may draw excessive current and get damaged. A series resistor limits that current. 💡





3. 🔌 How does a resistor work?
A resistor opposes the flow of current. For an ordinary fixed resistor, when its resistance increases and the supply voltage stays constant, the current decreases.
Ohm's Law explains the relationship:

V = IR


I = V/R



Where:
- \(V\) = Voltage in volts
- \(I\) = Current in amperes
- \(R\) = Resistance in ohms





4. 📏 What is resistance?
Resistance is the opposition a material or component provides to electric current.
- Symbol: R
- Unit: Ohm 
- Higher resistance generally means lower current at the same applied voltage.
- Lower resistance generally means higher current at the same applied voltage.

Common resistance units:

Unit	Equivalent
1 ohm (Ω)	1 Ω
1 kilo-ohm (kΩ)	1,000 Ω
1 mega-ohm (MΩ)	1,000,000 Ω
For example 4.7kohm = 4,700 ohm





5. 🌈 What is resistor colour coding?
Resistors often have coloured bands printed on their bodies. These bands tell you the resistor's resistance value and tolerance.
Colour coding is useful because tiny resistors may not have enough space to print their values as numbers.

Tolerance tells you how much the actual resistance may differ from its marked nominal value.

How to read a 4-band resistor
A typical 4-band resistor uses:
1. First band — first digit
2. Second band — second digit
3. Third band — multiplier
4. Fourth band — tolerance

Example: Brown – Black – Red – Gold

- Brown = 1
- Black = 0
- Red = ×100
- Gold = ±5%

Calculation:

R = 10*100=1000ohm

Therefore, the resistor is 1 kΩ ±5%.
Its actual resistance may typically lie between 950 Ω and 1,050 Ω.





6. 🧰 Types of resistors
Resistors can be classified as fixed, variable, or special-purpose resistors.









1. Fixed resistor
Its resistance is designed to remain approximately constant under specified operating conditions.
- Carbon film
- Metal film
- Metal oxide
- Wire-wound
📌 Example: A 1 kΩ resistor used in a circuit.



2. Variable resistor
Its resistance or output voltage can be adjusted.
- Potentiometer
- Rheostat
- Trimmer resistor
📌 Example: A potentiometer used to adjust volume or set a control signal.




3. Thermistor
Its resistance changes significantly with temperature.
- NTC: resistance decreases as temperature increases.
- PTC: resistance increases as temperature increases over its relevant operating range.
📌 Example: Temperature sensing and protection.




4. LDR (Light Dependent Resistor)
Its resistance changes according to the intensity of light. It typically decreases when light intensity increases.
📌 Example: Automatic streetlights.




5. Power resistor
Designed to dissipate comparatively higher amounts of electrical power.
📌 Example: Braking circuits, current limiting, and power supplies.
