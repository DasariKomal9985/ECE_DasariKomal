⚡ Capacitor 
1. 🔋 What is a capacitor?
A capacitor is an electronic component that stores electrical energy in an electric field and releases it when required.



2. 📘 Basic Concepts
- Symbol: C
- Unit: Farad (F)
- Measuring instrument: Multimeter with capacitance mode
- Main function: Store and release electrical energy.
- Construction: Usually consists of two conductive plates separated by an insulating material called a dielectric.



3. 🤔 Why do we use capacitors?
- 🔋 Store electrical energy temporarily.
- ⚡ Smooth voltage fluctuations in power supplies.
- 🔇 Filter unwanted electrical noise.
- ⏱️ Create timing and delay circuits.
- 🔄 Help start certain single-phase motors.
- 🔌 Couple AC signals while blocking steady DC in suitable circuits.



4. 🧰 Types of capacitors

1. Electrolytic capacitor
- Usually has high capacitance.
- Commonly polarized (+ and −).
- Used in power supplies and voltage smoothing.



2. Ceramic capacitor
- Small and commonly non-polarized.
- Used for filtering and noise suppression.



3. Film capacitor
- Generally non-polarized.
- Used in filtering, timing, and AC applications.



4. Motor capacitor
- Used in suitable single-phase motors.
- Start capacitors assist starting; run capacitors help maintain motor operation.




5. 🧮 Important formula
Capacitance is calculated using:

C = Q/V



Where:
- \(C\) = Capacitance in farads (F)
- \(Q\) = Stored charge in coulombs (C)
- \(V\) = Voltage across the capacitor (V)




Example: If a capacitor stores 10 coulombs of charge at 5 V:

C = 10/5 = 2F 

Therefore, the capacitance is 2 farads.


6. 🏭 Capacitors in industrial applications
- 🔌 Power supplies: Smooth DC voltage.
- ⚙️ Single-phase motors: Assist starting or running.
- 🎛️ Control circuits: Provide timing and filtering.
- ⚡ Power-factor correction: Capacitor banks help improve power factor in AC installations.
