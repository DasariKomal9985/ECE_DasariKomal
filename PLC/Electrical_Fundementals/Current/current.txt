⚡ What is Current?
Definition: Electric current is the rate at which electric charge flows through a conductor or electrical circuit.
In simple words, current tells us how much electric charge flows through a circuit per second.




Symbol and Unit
- Symbol: \(I\)
- SI unit: Ampere (A)
- Measuring instrument: Ammeter


Formula
I = Q/t

Where:
- \(I\) = Current (amperes)
- \(Q\) = Electric charge (coulombs)
- \(t\) = Time (seconds)



Example:
If 10 coulombs of charge flow through a wire in 2 seconds:

I = 10/2 ie: 5A..

Therefore, the current is 5 amperes.
Types of Current




DC — Direct Current
Current flows in one direction.
Example: batteries and 24 V DC PLC control circuits.





AC — Alternating Current
Current periodically changes direction.
Example: household electrical supply and industrial AC motors.



What determines the current?
According to Ohm's Law:

I = V/R

Where \(V\) is voltage and \(R\) is resistance.
For a fixed resistance:
- Higher voltage → higher current.
- Lower voltage → lower current.


Industrial example 🏭
Suppose a 24 V DC device has a resistance of 12 Ω.

I = 24/12 ie: 2A

The current is 2 amperes.

In a PLC control panel, current flows through devices such as sensors, relay coils and other electrical loads when their circuits are energized.

Remember: Voltage is the electrical potential difference, current is the rate of charge flow, and resistance opposes current flow. ⚡
