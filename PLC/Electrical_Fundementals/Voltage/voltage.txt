⚡ Voltage
1. What is Voltage?
Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points in an electrical circuit. It provides the push that drives electric current through a circuit. ⚡





2. 📘 Basic Concepts
- Symbol: V
- SI Unit: Volt (V)
- Measuring Instrument: Voltmeter
- Measurement: A voltmeter is connected in parallel across a component or power supply.
- Common units:
  - 1 millivolt (mV) = 0.001 V
  - 1 volt (V) = 1,000 mV
  - 1 kilovolt (kV) = 1,000 V




3. 🔋 Types of Voltage
1. DC Voltage (Direct Current) 🔋
- Polarity remains the same: positive (+) and negative (−).
- Examples: batteries, 12 V DC supplies, and 24 V DC PLC control circuits.
- Commonly used to power industrial sensors and control systems.
2. AC Voltage (Alternating Current) 🏠
- Voltage changes its polarity periodically.
- Examples: household electricity and industrial AC power supplies.
- Commonly used to operate AC motors and industrial machines.





4. 🔌 Relationship Between Voltage, Current and Resistance
According to Ohm’s Law:

V = I*R

Where:
- \(V\) = Voltage in volts (V)
- \(I\) = Current in amperes (A)
- \(R\) = Resistance in ohms (Ω)
Example: Suppose a circuit has a current of 2 A and a resistance of 6 Ω.

V = 2*6 = 12V

Therefore, the voltage is 12 V. ✅





5. 💡 How Voltage Works in a PLC Panel
Imagine a PLC control panel in an industrial machine:
- 🔌 The power supply receives 230 V AC.
- ⚙️ The SMPS converts it into 24 V DC.
- 📡 Sensors receive 24 V DC to operate.
- 🧠 The PLC reads the sensor signals.
- 🔄 The PLC output may control a relay or contactor, which switches a machine load.
Important: Voltage alone does not determine the current. Current also depends on the load’s resistance or electrical characteristics.





6. ⚠️ Important Points to Remember
- Higher voltage can drive more current through the same fixed resistance.
- If resistance increases while voltage remains constant, current decreases.
- Voltage is measured in volts; current is measured in amperes.
- Never measure voltage by connecting a multimeter in current mode across a power supply. This can cause a short circuit and damage the meter.
- ⚡ Industrial voltages can be dangerous. Always follow electrical safety procedures.




📝 One-line definition: Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points that drives electric current through a circuit.
